# Skylands

Skylands is an incremental city builder game about developing cities on flying islands across repeated runs. This context captures the shared domain language for gameplay concepts.

## Language

**Run**:
A single play session in which the player expands a city until SkyCoin reaches zero or below. A run starts with many visible flying islands whose whole area is buildable.
_Avoid_: Attempt, round, match

**Flying Island**:
A bounded landmass in the sky that provides a tile grid for city expansion. Flying islands may be procedurally generated to avoid a fixed space limit and can have height differences.
_Avoid_: Map, level, plot

**Empty Tile**:
A tile between flying islands. Only roads can be built on empty tiles, and roads built there are more expensive than roads built on island tiles.
_Avoid_: Void tile, gap

**City**:
The set of buildings the player places on flying islands during a run. The city resets at the start of each run.
_Avoid_: Base, settlement, town

**City Core**:
The fixed, unique, free starting building for each run and the origin of the city's road network. The City Core starts on a generated starting island in a fixed valid position chosen by the generator and provides a small baseline citizen capacity.
_Avoid_: Town hall, hub, capital

**Buildable Tile**:
A tile on a flying island where a building can be placed.
_Avoid_: Plot, cell, square

**Building**:
A city element that occupies tiles on a flying island and has a SkyCoin cost. In the first playable version, buildings are placed instantly from a toolbar with a two-by-two footprint preview, can be placed while disconnected, require all footprint tiles to be buildable and at the same height, have a SkyCoin cost that increases by count for each building type, and can be demolished with no refund; inactive buildings produce nothing and consume no operational resources. Building counts and cost scaling reset each run.
_Avoid_: Construction, structure

**Building Category**:
A high-level role for a building. The initial categories are Residential, Production, Utility, Knowledge, Transport, Extraction, and Road.
_Avoid_: Building type, class

**First Playable Building**:
One of the buildings included in the first playable version: City Core, Road, House, Farm, Workshop, Market, or Monument.
_Avoid_: Starter building, basic building

**Building Complexity**:
A value derived from a building's tier. More advanced connected buildings increase performance more.
_Avoid_: Complexity score, sophistication

**Game Tone**:
The intended feel of Skylands: heavily meme-inspired light satirical fantasy with playful names.
_Avoid_: Cozy realism, serious simulation

**SkyCoin**:
The run-limited money resource that is spent on buildings and decreases over time until it can end the run. SkyCoin drain increases over the course of a run, and the UI shows SkyCoin drain, tax income, and net SkyCoin per second separately.
_Avoid_: Money, cash, gold, currency

**Escalation**:
A minute-based increase in base SkyCoin drain during a run, becoming exponential in the late game. Bonuses can delay escalation but should not make runs endless.
_Avoid_: Difficulty, timer

**Energy**:
A citizen need and city capacity produced by some buildings. Buildings may require Energy to operate, and excess Energy contributes to performance.
_Avoid_: Power, electricity

**Food**:
A capped, stocked citizen need produced per second by farms and consumed per second by citizens. Food is the only explicit need in the first playable version; when Food reaches zero, citizen arrival stops but tax income is not reduced in the first playable version. The UI shows Food stock, cap, production per second, consumption per second, and net Food per second.
_Avoid_: Meals, supplies

**Knowledge**:
A citizen need and city output that unlocks new building types and discoveries during a run and contributes to productivity.
_Avoid_: Research, science

**Citizen**:
A person living in the city, represented as an abstract population count. Citizens arrive at a base rate while Food is available and citizen capacity remains; they generate tax income, fill jobs in later versions, increase their needs as their count grows, and contribute strongly to performance.
_Avoid_: Population, resident, worker

**Tax Income**:
SkyCoin generated by citizens based on citizen count and multiplied by productivity. Tax income can exceed SkyCoin drain during parts of a run until escalation overtakes it.
_Avoid_: Revenue, salary

**Need**:
A citizen requirement measured as a city capacity. The initial needs are Food, Energy, Transport, and Knowledge; unmet needs stop citizen arrival and may reduce productivity in later versions, but citizens do not leave in the first version.
_Avoid_: Service, demand

**Job**:
A role provided by a building and filled by a citizen. Jobs are deferred from the first playable version.
_Avoid_: Work slot, assignment

**Residential Building**:
A building that adds citizen capacity. Citizens move in over time when the city has enough connected services.
_Avoid_: Housing, home

**Transport**:
The city's ability to move citizens between connected buildings, and one of the citizen needs.
_Avoid_: Logistics, transit

**Road**:
A one-tile placeable connection that links buildings into the city, cannot overlap buildings, and must be demolished before a building can be placed on its tile. Roads can be placed by dragging a path and paying per valid tile; the UI shows path validity and total SkyCoin cost before placement, and the whole path is rejected if total cost is unaffordable. A building is connected if any tile of its footprint is orthogonally adjacent to a road connected to the City Core. Disconnected buildings produce no outputs, and roads can be demolished with no refund.
_Avoid_: Path, street

**Island Road**:
A road built on a flying island tile with the normal SkyCoin cost. Island Roads can connect adjacent island tiles only when their height difference is one level or less.
_Avoid_: Ground road

**Sky Road**:
A road built on an empty tile between flying islands with an elevated SkyCoin cost. Sky Roads can turn like normal roads and can connect across empty tiles only when the landing tile is within one height level of the starting tile.
_Avoid_: Bridge, floating road

**Ramp Road**:
An Island Road connecting adjacent island tiles with different heights.
_Avoid_: Slope, stair

**Productivity**:
A city multiplier that slightly improves produced outputs and contributes to performance. Bonuses can increase productivity.
_Avoid_: Efficiency, citizen performance

**Discovery**:
A run-limited unlock found through Knowledge. Discoveries can reveal ores, enable new buildings, grant in-run passive effects, and contribute to performance.
_Avoid_: Bonus, research unlock

**Ore**:
A run resource such as Bronze, Silver, Gold, Coal, Uranium, or HolyGold. Ores are discovered during a run, extracted from ore sources, accumulated, and can be required to build or operate certain buildings.
_Avoid_: Mineral, metal

**Ore Source**:
A visible source of a specific ore on a flying island. Ore extractor buildings produce ore when placed on or near a matching ore source and staffed by citizens.
_Avoid_: Deposit, vein

**Milestone**:
A predefined run achievement, such as reaching a citizen count, extracting an ore, or placing an advanced building. Milestones are one-time per run, reset each run, and contribute to performance, but do not grant immediate rewards in the first playable version; the game persistently tracks how many times each milestone has been reached.
_Avoid_: Achievement, objective

**Performance**:
The visible run evaluation of the city, accumulating over time from active sources and milestones. Performance is based on peak achievements during the run, including highest citizen count, highest production capacities, highest building complexity, and new milestones reached.
_Avoid_: Score, rating

**DivineCoin**:
The persistent resource earned through a nonlinear prestige formula from performance when a run ends or when the player uses Task Failed Successfully, and spent on bonuses. Retiring immediately awards zero unless the city has reached a meaningful performance threshold.
_Avoid_: DivideCoin, prestige currency

**Milestone Diminishing Return**:
A reduction in DivineCoin value for repeatedly reaching the same milestone across runs. This affects DivineCoin awards, not visible performance.
_Avoid_: Milestone penalty, score decay

**Bonus**:
A persistent between-run improvement the player buys with DivineCoin to change future runs. Bonuses have multiple levels with increasing DivineCoin costs and are required for progression; they can improve starting SkyCoin, reduce SkyCoin drain, improve productivity, increase starting Knowledge, or improve DivineCoin gain, but should not directly place buildings or make runs endless. The first successful run should afford Stonks !, the cheapest bonus, if the player does not retire too early.
_Avoid_: Upgrade, perk, power-up

**First Playable Bonus**:
One of the bonuses included in the first playable version: Stonks ! for more starting SkyCoin, Long Suffering for slower SkyCoin drain, or Drug Traffic for higher productivity.
_Avoid_: Starter bonus, basic bonus

**Task Failed Successfully**:
The voluntary early run-ending action that converts current performance into DivineCoin.
_Avoid_: Retire, cash out

**I Declare Bankruptcy**:
The forced run-ending event when SkyCoin reaches zero or below, converting performance into DivineCoin.
_Avoid_: Bankruptcy, defeat

**Workshop**:
A first playable building that produces a run-lifetime performance contribution over time while active, affected by productivity.
_Avoid_: Factory

**Market**:
A first playable building that adds productivity.
_Avoid_: Bazaar

**Monument**:
An expensive late-run SkyCoin sink that adds performance and can trigger milestones, but does not help the city survive.
_Avoid_: Statue

**First Playable Version**:
The smallest version that proves the core game loop. It includes SkyCoin, DivineCoin, Citizens, Food, Roads, City Core, House, Farm, Workshop, Market, Monument, three Bonuses, Milestones, a City screen, and a between-run Bonus screen; it excludes Jobs, Ores, Energy, Knowledge, Transport as explicit systems, and offline run progress.
_Avoid_: MVP, prototype, v0

**First Playable Milestone**:
One of the milestones included in the first playable version: First House, 10 Citizens, First Market, First Monument, 100 Performance, two-minute survival, or ten-minute survival.
_Avoid_: Starter milestone, basic milestone

**City Screen**:
The run screen where the player navigates a 3D isometric tile grid with pan, zoom, and free rotation; places buildings; places roads; demolishes; inspects tiles and buildings; watches live resources; pauses the run; and can use Task Failed Successfully. Paused mode allows City Screen inspection only; building, demolishing, Bonus Screen access, and Task Failed Successfully require normal speed.
_Avoid_: Main screen, game screen

**Simulation Tick**:
A one-second discrete simulation step used to advance runs. Rates are displayed to the player as per-second values.
_Avoid_: Frame, realtime update

**Primary Input**:
The first playable version's input assumption: mouse is required, and keyboard shortcuts are optional polish.
_Avoid_: Control scheme, bindings

**Building Modelizer**:
A development tool for assembling simple building models cube by cube. It is not part of the first playable game.
_Avoid_: Model editor, asset editor

**Bonus Screen**:
The between-run screen where the player spends DivineCoin on bonuses.
_Avoid_: Upgrade screen, shop

**Save State**:
The persistent state kept between sessions: DivineCoin, bonus levels, milestone reach counts, and the current run if one is in progress. Time does not advance while the game is closed.
_Avoid_: Save file, profile

**Invalid Placement**:
A blocked placement attempt. In the first playable version, placement is invalid if it is occupied, off-grid, unaffordable, places a building footprint on empty tiles, or places a road over a building.
_Avoid_: Placement error, blocked tile
